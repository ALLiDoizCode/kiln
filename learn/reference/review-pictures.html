Review pictures

The eight pictures kiln renders for a review: where each camera stands, and what the picture is for.

Run it

python3 -m kiln run --model rock.glb --size 0.8 --profile pit ...    renders them and stops
python3 -m kiln review rock                                          says where they are
python3 -m kiln review rock --approve     or     --reject            records the decision

They are written to assets/<name>/review/shape/<view>.png, with a page beside them, index.html, that shows the reference image next to every picture. The renderer can also be run by itself:

cargo build --release -p asset_view --bin review_pictures
target/release/review_pictures rock.glb --size 0.8 --closest 0.5 --out pictures/

How the model is placed

The model file is only read. For the pictures the model is put in front of the cameras like this:

StepWhat is doneWhy
ScaleOne factor on all three axes, so the largest dimension of the bounding box is the run's size.A raw output arrives at any scale. The pictures must show the asset as large as it will be.
Stand on the groundMoved so the lowest point of the bounding box is at height 0.A ground plane is drawn at height 0. The shadow on it shows whether the model rests flat.
CentreMoved so the middle of the bounding box is over the origin.Every camera aims at the same place whatever offset the file has.
TurnNothing. The model keeps the orientation it has in the file.A model that lies on its side or faces backwards is a fault the review should show.

The bounding box is taken over every vertex where the scene places it, as in Measuring a model.

Axes and names

AxisIn glTF and in Bevy
+YUp.
+ZThe way the front of a model faces. A camera that looks at the front stands on +Z and looks towards -Z.
+XTo the right in the front picture.

right and left are named as Blender names its views: by the side of the front picture they are taken from, not by the model's own right and left hand.

The set

Every picture is 1920 × 1080 pixels. "Size" below is the run's size, so the first six distances grow with the asset and it fills the same share of the picture whatever it is. The last two are fixed distances in metres, as a player meets the asset.

NameWhere the camera standsLooks atField of viewWhat it is for
front3 sizes from the middle of the box, on +ZThe middle of the box30°The outline to compare with the reference image, if that shows the front.
right3 sizes, on +XThe middle30°The outline from the side.
back3 sizes, on -ZThe middle30°What the reference image does not show. Generators guess it.
left3 sizes, on -XThe middle30°The other side.
top3 sizes, straight aboveThe middle; the front is towards the bottom of the picture30°The footprint, and holes or mess on top.
three_quarter3.5 sizes, half way between front and right, 30° above levelThe middle30°The form as a whole: three faces at once, lit from the side.
standing2 m out from the front of the box, 1.7 m above the groundThe middle72°Whether the size is right. A post 1.8 m tall, as tall as a player, stands 0.3 m to the right of the box.
closestThe target profile's closest viewing distance out from the front of the box (0.5 m for the pit profile), at 1.7 m above the ground or at the middle of the asset's height if that is lowerLevel, straight at the front72°The surface as the player sees it from as near as the game allows. The asset need not fit in the picture.

"Field of view" is the angle from the top of the picture to the bottom. The "front of the box" is the face of the bounding box on +Z, so the surface itself is at that distance or a little further.

The numbers and where they come from

NumberValueFrom
Closest viewing distance0.5 mclosest_viewing_distance in the target profile. For the pit profile it is the distance the budget measurement assumed, and a placeholder until the profile's fields are settled.
Eye height1.7 mThe budget measurement: a player 1.8 m tall.
Player's field of view72°The budget measurement.
Picture size1920 × 1080The budget measurement. With the field of view, it makes a pixel of closest and standing a pixel of the game.
Field of view of the whole pictures30°Chosen here. A narrow angle from further away draws the near and far parts of the asset at nearly the same scale, so an outline can be compared with a reference image.
3 sizesChosen here. A cube as large as the size is three quarters of the picture's height; nothing of the size reaches the edge.
3.5 sizesChosen here. From a corner the diagonal of the box has to fit: a cube takes about 85% of the height.
2 m, and the postChosen here. Near enough to judge the size against the post, far enough that a prop is seen whole.

The eye height, field of view and picture size are assumptions of learn/research/pit-budget-measurement.md, section 2. They are written into the renderer, not read from the target profile, until the profile holds them.

Light and surroundings

ThingWhat it isWhat to expect in the pictures
SunOne directional light from above, in front and to the left. It does not move with the camera.front, left and top are lit. back and right show the shaded sides, lit only by the ambient light.
Ambient lightThe same from every direction.Shaded sides stay readable, with less contrast.
GroundA flat grey plane at height 0.The cast shadow falls behind and to the right.
BackgroundOne flat grey.

The scene and the light are the viewer's (cargo run -p asset_view), so a picture shows what the viewer shows. The pictures use a finer shadow map than the viewer, so that a shadow's edge is not drawn in steps.

The same pictures every time

What is fixedHow
Cameras, light, background, picture sizeConstants. The camera positions follow only from the size, the bounding box and the closest viewing distance.
TimeNothing in the scene moves. Each picture is taken a set number of frames after its camera is placed.
LoadingNo picture is taken until the model and its textures have loaded, and every shader is compiled before the frame that uses it. A model that does not load ends the renderer with an error and no pictures.

Measured on the development machine (RTX 3080, Vulkan): two specimens and two textured test models, five renders each, gave the same bytes for every picture every time. Another graphics card or driver may round differently. The asset record therefore keeps the pictures' checksums only inside the review, to say which pictures a decision was made on. Nothing that kiln rebuild compares depends on them.

What the asset record keeps

"shape_review": {
  "decision": "approved",            null while the review waits; or "rejected"
  "note": "matches the picture",     or null
  "decided_on": "2026-10-07",        the day of the decision
  "pictures": {
    "view_set": 1,                   goes up whenever a camera of the set changes
    "closest_viewing_distance": 0.5,
    "picture_size": [1920, 1080],
    "renderer": { the program, its version, Bevy's version, the graphics card },
    "views": [ {"name": "front", "path": "review/shape/front.png", "sha256": "..."}, ... ]
  }
}

Pictures with different view_set numbers cannot be compared with each other. The pictures themselves are not in git.
