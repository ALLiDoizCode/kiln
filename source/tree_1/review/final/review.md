# tree_1, tree_2, tree_3 / final: review

Two reviewers that did not build the trees, on 2026-10-05, each given only the briefs, specs, sheets and tiles, reports and exported files (the `asset-review` skill). **A** asked whether each tree matches its brief; **B** checked the skill's list of smells. This file covers all three variants; `tree_2` and `tree_3` point here.

The measurements are the reviewers'. The reconciling agent looked at `benchmarks/out/tree_stages.png` and `tree_seasons.png` and saw the plinth-like foot, the pale pieces under `tree_1` and the dark underside there; it re-measured nothing else. No finding was shown to be wrong, so every one is accepted. Approval is the owner's and has not been given.

**Outcomes, 2026-10-05 (branch `treefix`).** The owner answered both questions: limbs are to stand out from the leaves, by lightening the leaf undersides and not the bark; `tree_1` keeps its shape and its sentence is corrected. Findings 3, 4 and 6 to 9 are closed. Findings 1, 2, 5 and 10 are **not done**: what was tried and what is known is in their rows. The Outcome column below says where each stands. A second attempt at 1 and 5, the same day, is parked on the local branch `treefix-foot-wip` (three commits on top of this branch, not green at L4) and is described in rows 1, 2 and 5 under "Second attempt".

**Outcomes, 2026-10-05 (branch `treefoot`).** Finding 1 is done on eight of the nine trees and finding 5 is fixed; finding 2 is not started. The swept foot first failed the load test's crevice check on three trees. The cause was measured on `tree_2`, with the painter's shadow baked alone: the painter's shadow is whole where a fifth of the sky above a face is hidden, which a fold of 53 degrees does at its line, while the load test called every fold of 30 degrees a crevice. The root valleys are folded 30 to 50 degrees: the painter gave them 0.18 to 0.52 of the shadow and the load test asked half of it or more (`painted.crevices_darker` 0.74 and 0.75 against 0.70). Both now read one definition from `conventions.toml` (`crevice_sky_hidden`, `join_sky_hidden`); no texture changed for it. The earlier reading, that shaded valley faces were counted as open faces, was wrong: no open sample had any shadow (0.000 on all four trees measured).

**Later the same day (branch `treefoot`).** `tree_young_1` is through its gate. `painted.colour` failed it at 0.915 with the paint right, because grained bark is two tones, furrows at 0.59 and plates at 1.29 of the tone without grain, each about half the surface, and over a tree's few open faces their shares do not average out. The check now holds a grained surface's furrows and plates each to its own tone (twelve trees, old foot and new: 1.013 to 1.029 and 0.967 to 1.014; bark a fifth too dark: 0.844 and 0.856). For the owner: the furrows cover 0.40 to 0.50 of the open bark, which is not the "dark furrows between plates" of ADR 12 read literally; the look is the one chosen there, and is not changed.

## Retrospective

Not done in full: it follows the owner's verdict. Already known from this review, to hand to the `asset-checks` skill: findings 3 and 4 were caught by an eye and a number could have caught both (a leaf sample from below far lighter than the lightest underside swatch; bark and leaf value from below closer than a set ratio), so both are escaped defects. Findings 1 and 2 are a starved silhouette that the existing flare and root checks pass; whether a number can catch them is open.

Added with the outcomes of 2026-10-05:

- Finding 3's check was written before its red case was run, against the `asset-checks` loop. Its first form counted leaf samples lighter than the palette allows on the side the sun cannot reach. When the case ran (the leaf material with its gloss back), it was NOT CAUGHT: 0.00055 against 0.00030 on the fixed tree, and its limit had by then been raised from 0.0002 to 0.001 to let the fixed trees through. The check was rebuilt on what the defect is (grey, not a leaf's colour) and then separated the two by a factor of 200. A limit moved until the asset passes is the tautology the skill warns of; the red case is what showed it.
- The likely cause the review named for finding 3 was wrong, and so was the first fix made on it. Tracing the pale pixels back to their faces showed most of them on faces the sun does reach.
- Findings 1 and 2: still open whether a number can catch them. Later the same day: a number does catch finding 1 (`roots_apart`, above; the plinth's sides between root tips are what it measures), and the curve only weakly. Finding 2 has a candidate that was measured and not built as a check: the largest bend between the middles of successive rings of a limb.
- The second attempt at the foot was judged on the shape gates and a picture before the painted gates were run on every tree. `tree_1` passed them; three other trees did not. A change to a generator is not known to be green until every tree it draws has been through L4.
- The numbers in the Outcome column for findings 3 and 4 were measured under the viewer's ambient light of 300. Master then raised it to 900 and the gate became L4e; the limits were measured again on all eight trees, unfixed and fixed, and are in the family brief (Decisions). The red-first run was then done as the skill asks, each check stubbed to pass: the cases for `view.under_pale` and `view.under_limbs` failed as they should, and the case for `view.under_seen` passed with its check stubbed, because the other two checks' messages named its id. Their messages were changed and the case then failed with the stub and passes without it.
- Branch `treefoot`: the first reading of the failing crevice check (shaded faces counted as open) was a guess from the totals. Baking the painter's shadow by itself and reading it at the load test's own samples refuted it in one run, and showed two separate causes: two definitions of a crevice, and grain that does not average out over a tree's few open faces. The second is a weakness of `painted.colour` on any asset with grain: ten trees read 0.915 to 0.998 where 0.92 is the floor, by which faces happen to be open.
