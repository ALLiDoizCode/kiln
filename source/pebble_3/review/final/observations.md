# pebble_3, final: what the sheet shows

The second pebble: redone lower and rounder after the owner's first review. Read from `sheet.png`, `benchmarks/out/pebble_variants.png` and `benchmarks/out/pebble_3_candidates.png`. Numbers in brackets are the gate's own, from `out/reports/`. The value map and squint aids were not remade for this version: items 9 and 10 are read from the plain tiles and say so.

1. **Silhouette.** Low: `front` and `back`: a flat-topped trapezoid about 4.9 times as wide as tall, its top a nearly level line over about two thirds of the width; `right`: the same, 3.8 times as wide as tall. Rounded: `top`: an uneven outline of seven straight sides with blunt corners, the longest side about twice the shortest; no view shows a point, a vertical wall or one large side face. Settled: in `front`, `right` and `back` the sides meet the ground leaning in at about 55 to 70 degrees, with no gap and no undercut.
2. **Proportions.** 0.5 x 0.38 x 0.1 m: 0.200 as tall as its wider side [brief: at most 0.25; the first pebbles 0.32 to 0.42]. The largest steep plane holds 0.045 of the surface seen, at 45 degrees from level [at most 0.07; the first pebbles 0.10 to 0.16]. It holds 0.513 of its bounding box [at least 0.4]; the slice three quarters of the way up is 0.374 of the footprint [at least 0.2]; none of the side surface is within 8 degrees of upright [at most 0.15]; the summit is 0.187 of the half extents off the middle [at least 0.15].
3. **Facing and grounding.** The base sits on the bottom edge of the frame in `front`, `right` and `back`; no gap shows under it. A pebble has no front.
4. **Topology** (clay_wire). 122 triangles [budget 150]. `top` shows three rings: the cap, about a third of the outline's area; a plane of the rim over each side, together a band about a quarter of the stone's width wide; and seven sides, a narrow border. Every plane edge carries one bevel strip, a thin double line. The cap is a fan of long thin triangles from one corner; none supports a change of outline.
5. **Shading.** No face is darker or lighter than its neighbours without a lighting reason in any material tile; no hard edge shows above the ground [load test: none].
6. **Materials.** One grey. The top is a little lighter than the foot in the side views [lowest quarter of the open faces 0.967 where the tints give 0.917 times the highest]: the stone is so low that the gradient from ground to top is only a few percent on its open faces, weaker than the mottling [tone spread 0.24 for a blotch of 0.12], which is the strongest thing on the stone in `top`. Edges are lighter than the planes [exposed edges 1.29 times open faces]; in `top` this shows as a pale line round the cap and along the rim's lower edge. No crevice shadow: no inside corner [0 samples].
7. **Scale** (scale tiles). About an eighteenth of the figure's height [brief: one eighteenth of 1.8 m].
8. **In the engine** (bevy, bevy_back; `pebble_variants.png`, `pebble_3_candidates.png`). In `--close` the lit cap and rim are one pale mass and only the sides facing away are darker, a narrow dark band at the foot on one side; the big dark cut face of the first pebbles is gone from all twelve seeds on the candidates sheet. The pebble casts almost no shadow on the ground at this height. The mottling is stronger under Bevy than the edge light, as before.
9. **Values** (plain tiles, no value map made). Two masses: the pale top (cap and rim together) and the narrow darker sides; on the review ground the top is close to the backdrop's value. The benchmark shows three: pale top, lighter rim line, green mossy foot.
10. **At a glance** (plain tiles, no squint view made). "Flat grey pebble." The benchmark reads as "flat mossy pebble".
11. **Differences from the brief.**
    - Its largest steep plane (0.045 of the surface) is a plane of the rim at 45 degrees and not a side: stretching the plate to its bounds steepened that rim plane past the 24 to 36 degrees it was drawn at.
    - The three are one construction, and it shows: every seed is a plate with a rim that starts at one level all the way round, like a cut gem. Beside the benchmark (`Pebble_Round_1`, 0.50 x 0.37 x 0.10 m, 136 triangles) the proportion now matches (0.2 against 0.19), but the benchmark's outline is a bean with a hollow side and ours is always convex, and its rim carries a drawn light line and moss that ours has not. The brief leaves growth out on purpose.
    - The cap is one plane and about a third of the top; the benchmark's top is several nearly level planes. Seen from above, about a third of our stone is within 20 degrees of level against about half of the benchmark's (measured once with a scratch script, not a gate).
    - Silhouette 6 (the count of planes) has no check; it reads as described in item 4.
    - The gradient is weak on a stone this low (item 6), and `painted.gradient` then turns on the mottling: it failed on pebble_1 with seed 1. A low stone may need the check or the paint looked at; neither was changed.
    - On green or dark ground the pale top may read differently; it was not tried.
