# pebble_2, final: what the sheet shows

Read from `sheet.png`, the aids of the `material_three_quarter` and `bevy` tiles, and `benchmarks/out/pebble_variants.png`. Numbers in brackets are the gate's own, from `out/reports/`.

1. **Silhouette.** Small and low: `front` and `back`: a low mound, about 2.8 times as wide as tall, highest left of the middle in `front`; `right`: a flat-topped block 2.2 times as wide as tall, its top a level line over three fifths of the width. `top`: seven straight sides. Rounded: no view shows a point or a vertical wall; every corner of the outline is blunt, cut by a plane or a soft edge. Settled into the ground: in `front`, `right` and `back` the sides meet the ground leaning in at 45 to 70 degrees, and none is undercut where it can be seen, so the stone reads as a mound on the ground and the brief's "cut where it still widens" does not show from these views.
2. **Proportions.** 0.25 x 0.2 x 0.09 m. It holds 0.522 of its bounding box [brief: at least 0.4]; the slice three quarters of the way up is 0.480 of the footprint [at least 0.2]; 0.045 of the side surface is within 8 degrees of upright [at most 0.15]; the summit is 0.218 of the half extents off the middle [at least 0.15].
3. **Facing and grounding.** The base sits on the bottom edge of the frame in `front`, `right` and `back`; no gap shows under it. A pebble has no front.
4. **Topology** (clay_wire). 92 triangles [budget 150]. `top` shows a cap that fills about half the outline, three shoulders and five sides. The cap is larger here than on the other two, and the stone is the fullest of the three. Every plane edge carries one bevel strip, a thin double line in the wire tiles. The planes are fans of long thin triangles with no interior vertex; none supports a change of outline.
5. **Shading.** No face is darker or lighter than its neighbours without a lighting reason in any material tile; no hard edge shows above the ground [load test: none]. Each plane is lit flat and the strips light as rounded edges.
6. **Materials.** One grey. The top is lighter than the foot in every side view [lowest quarter of the open faces 0.627 where the tints give 0.632 times the highest]. The planes are mottled in patches about a tenth of the width across [tone spread 0.24 for a blotch of 0.12]. Edges are lighter than the planes by a little [exposed edges 1.24 times open faces]; in the material tiles this shows as a thin pale line on the upper edges and not at all on the lower ones. No crevice shadow anywhere: the load test found no inside corner to measure [0 samples].
7. **Scale** (scale tiles). About a twentieth of the figure's height [brief: one twentieth of 1.8 m].
8. **In the engine** (bevy, bevy_back; `pebble_variants.png`). In the `bevy` tile the stone is about a twentieth of the figure's height: its planes cannot be told apart there, only a light top and a darker side. In `--close` the same planes and tones as Blender's tiles; the engine's light makes the side facing away two values darker than the top, and the mottling is the strongest thing on the lit planes. The edge light is hard to see in Bevy from any of the five views.
9. **Values** (value map of `material_three_quarter`). Two masses: the lit top planes, which have the backdrop's own value and vanish into it, and the dark planes of the foot facing away. The benchmark pebble, in `pebble_variants.png --close`, shows three: a pale lilac top, a lighter rim, and a green mossy foot.
10. **At a glance** (squint). "Low grey stone." The eye lands on the dark foot. The benchmark reads as "flat mossy pebble" and the eye lands on its pale top inside its light rim.
11. **Differences from the brief.**
    - Silhouette 6: the cut below the widest part is not seen in any view (item 1). The sides that face down are few and meet the ground under the stone's own outline.
    - The fullest: its crown is 0.48 of the footprint, which in `right` reads as a flat top and nearer a block than a rounded stone.
    - Beside the benchmark (`Pebble_Round_1`, 0.50 x 0.37 x 0.10 m, 136 triangles) these are taller for their width (0.32 to 0.42 against 0.2), more angular, and plain: the benchmark is a flat plate with one broad bevelled rim, a drawn light line on that rim, and moss at the foot. The brief leaves growth out on purpose; the owner should see the comparison before that stands.
    - The edge light at one thirtieth of the width passes the load test and barely reads by eye (items 6 and 8).
    - Silhouette 5 (ten to sixteen planes of different sizes) has no check; it reads as described in item 4.
    - From 1.7 m and further the top has the review ground's value (item 9), so the stone reads mostly by its dark side and its cast shadow. On green or dark ground this may differ; it was not tried.
