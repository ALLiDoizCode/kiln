# 13. Assets come in families; a rock may be several overlapping pieces; variants are palettes

The target is the whole catalogue in `docs/style/catalogue.md`: about thirty-five families, each in several shapes, sizes or growth stages, seasons, colours and covers. Three decisions follow from that scale. The owner agreed to each on 2026-10-05.

**Families.** A family is one generator and one brief. Its assets are specs: a seed, a size or growth stage, and a palette. For trees, a species is a recipe the one tree generator reads (how the trunk forks, where the pads sit, the leaf shape), and a growth stage is a number that scales height, trunk thickness and pad count. A new tree is a new spec, not new code. This is cheap to design in now and expensive to retrofit after dozens of assets exist.

**Rocks of several pieces.** A rock asset may be several closed pieces that overlap, each given its soft edges on its own; painted crevice shadow hides the joins. Until now every asset had to be one closed skin, and the boulder showed what that costs: pieces fused by boolean union leave short edges along the joins that cannot be softened, only two of forty seeds built, and a convex-hull construction that made better shapes could not pass the load test at all. Arches, stacks, crags and spires are all several pieces. The rock reference appears to be built this way too: in its previews each block keeps its own edges and passes into its neighbours, and its average of about 280 triangles per rock is too low for fused clusters. That is a reading of pictures, not of the files.

The cost is hidden faces inside the overlaps, which spend triangles and texture on what is never seen. The checks must change to match: closed surface and outward normals are asked of each piece, and new checks limit how much surface is buried and require pieces to touch.

**Variants as palettes.** Season, colour and cover are changes of palette and painted shading on the same mesh wherever possible. Leaf colour is already a palette strip (ADR 11); snow and moss are growth on upward-facing surfaces (ADR 10). A variant then costs a texture and not a model. Winter-bare trees and snow deep enough to change the outline are separate meshes.
